RESEARCH NOTE · 10 NOVEMBER 2026

Attention as input.

An optional installation module for Living Space. Start with an exhibit that notices where you look; keep deliberate actions under your control.

Status: desk research and a pointer-dwell interaction prototype. No eye tracker, webcam inference, calibration or hardware measurements have been implemented or tested. The pointer-only Attention gallery prototype is parked outside the shipped playground until an actual eye-tracking implementation is ready.

Is it possible?

Yes. A webcam can estimate gaze on a screen after learning a mapping for the viewer; dedicated trackers offer another route. WebGazer runs in the browser and estimates screen positions from webcam features and calibration interactions. This is an estimate, not a precise replacement for a mouse. Brown WebGazer.

Our initial installation assumption is one consenting visitor in front of one known screen, in controlled lighting, with a visible start/stop flow and a physical or touch fallback. Free movement through a room, shared projections and multiple simultaneous viewers are separate research tracks. A 2D gaze point cannot identify which overlapping 3D layer the visitor intends to inspect; the interaction must resolve that ambiguity.

What we should make first

Recommendation: an attention-responsive exhibition, with three large, stable exhibits. A brief glance changes light or sharpness; sustained attention brings the work forward; a button or touch confirms deeper navigation. The parked pointer prototype tested only the dwell-to-focus part. Actual gaze should not freely drag panels or continuously orbit the camera.

  • Attention lens: gently reduce depth blur around the attended exhibit. Keep text and controls readable everywhere.
  • Quiet approach: after a stable dwell, raise one exhibit and reveal its context. Freeze its target zone during the transition so it does not escape the viewer's gaze.
  • Gaze to prepare, touch to commit: preview a chapter with gaze; use a physical button, touch or keyboard to enter it.
  • Stillness on attention: slow an ambient orbit while the visitor examines an object, then ease back after they leave. Use hysteresis so brief glances do not repeatedly stop and restart it.
  • Peripheral discovery: related artifacts appear softly in the periphery. They should invite a choice without continuously stealing attention.
  • Presence choreography: gather the installation when a visitor leaves. Head/presence sensing can drive this without claiming gaze accuracy.

These are design hypotheses, not validated findings. Avoid blink-to-click as the default, involuntary selection on every fixation, fast camera movement tied to eye motion, or interpretations of emotion or intent from gaze.

Keep the core small

The proposed attention module is a separate, optional layer. Living Space keeps ownership of spatial motion; the adapter supplies samples. Camera models, native SDKs and licenses remain outside the core package. An adapter boundary does not itself resolve third-party license obligations.

Camera or tracker
  → provider adapter
  → screen calibration / coordinate mapping
  → validity + freshness gate
  → smoothing + stable target selection
  → candidate / dwell / focus / leave
  → Strata focus and depth controls

Proposed sample contract: normalized viewport x/y, monotonic timestamp, valid, provider identity and calibration version. Add confidence only if the provider actually supplies a meaningful confidence signal; otherwise use measured validation quality. Expose start(), stop(), calibrate(), validate() and a sample subscription. This is a proposal, not a currently exported API.

A native tracker can feed a small local bridge that emits only mapped gaze samples over a loopback WebSocket. Bind locally, restrict origins and authenticate the session. Handle viewport offsets, device pixel ratio, fullscreen changes and multiple monitors explicitly. Recalibrate or invalidate the mapping when the display geometry changes. Do not send raw eye video through the bridge by default.

Run inference separately from rendering where the chosen runtime supports it. Consume the newest sample once per animation frame; drop stale samples instead of building a queue. Cache projected hit regions and resolve only visible, eligible targets. Keep an acquired target stable while its panel animates; do not let background surfaces steal selection through an overlapping foreground panel.

Interaction state and failure behavior

idle → candidate → dwell → focused → release, with unavailable as a first-class state. Suggested starting parameters are a 900ms dwell, 250ms stale-sample cutoff and a 400ms leave grace period. These are tuning values to test, not measured thresholds. Use separate entry and exit boundaries, a cooldown after acquisition and a clear dwell-progress cue.

Tracking loss, a blink, permission denial or leaving the tracking area must cancel pending activation. Hold the current readable state briefly, then return to a quiet overview. Never trigger an action on stale or invalid coordinates. Require deliberate confirmation for navigation or any consequential action. Escape and the physical/touch fallback must always work.

Installation protocol

  1. Invite: explain the experience and camera/tracker use before capture. One clear Start action; an equally clear Stop action. Camera capture requires browser permission and a secure context. W3C Media Capture and Streams.
  2. Align: establish the viewing position and correct screen. Give a simple tracking-quality cue without exposing a busy diagnostic interface.
  3. Calibrate: use a short multi-point task, then evaluate separate held-out points. Training fit is not validation.
  4. Adapt: enable only interactions supported by the measured quality. Fall back from fine zones to broad zones, then to pointer/touch.
  5. Reset between visitors: clear calibration and session state; stop camera tracks when the session ends. Avoid recording video or storing gaze trails in the normal experience. Keep diagnostic capture separately opt-in.

Provisional pilot targets: at least 90% correct acquisition for the three large zones after calibration, no stale-sample activation, no more than one unintended focus change in a five-minute viewing task, and a consistently usable fallback. Determine acceptable p95 latency and error from the intended screen size and viewing distance; do not substitute a vendor sample rate for end-to-end latency. None of these targets has yet been measured.

Next research decision

Prototype the broad-zone experience with a webcam first, then compare it with a development-appropriate screen tracker on the intended installation. Use wearable tracking only if movement through the space is essential. Keep the visual experience and provider interchangeable so the product does not become tied to one camera, tracker or license.
